# Scoring

> **Your master CV is the foundation. Write it yourself.**
> Every sub-score is judged against the evidence in `cv/master.md`. Evidence you didn't write down can't be scored. You are strongly encouraged to draft the master CV in your own words; `/cv-master` interviews you to surface what's missing.

## What the score measures

A **0-100 fit score**: how well your background matches the job **as written**.

It is **not** the probability of getting an interview. Referrals, timing, internal candidates, and ATS filters are invisible to it, on purpose. A score that tried to include them would be guessing, and you couldn't tell fit from luck when reviewing results.

**Use it to:**
- choose which roles are worth tailoring for
- see *why* a role fits or doesn't (the sub-scores matter more than the total)
- find the specific gaps to address in your CV, cover letter, or interview prep

**Don't use it to:** predict outcomes, until `/calibrate` shows it does for you.

## The rubric

Five sub-scores, each 0-100, weighted. Default weights are below; yours live in `profile/rubric.md` (setup step 2).

| # | Dimension | Default weight | Measures | Made specific to you by |
|---|-----------|----------------|----------|-------------------------|
| D1 | Domain match | 30 | Overlap between the role's core substance and your hands-on domains | Your core / adjacent / outside tiers |
| D2 | Requirement coverage | 25 | Share of stated must-haves you can evidence (met = 1, partial = 0.5, unmet = 0) | Your master CV |
| D3 | Seniority and scope | 20 | Fit between the role's level and yours; penalizes both under-leveling and reach | Your level band |
| D4 | Narrative fit | 15 | Whether your career throughline maps cleanly onto the role's central problem | Your throughline sentence |
| D5 | Gap severity (inverted) | 10 | 100 minus penalties for gaps | Your hard-gap list |

**Total** = sum of (weight × sub-score) / 100.

Every analysis shows all five. A 60 built from balanced dimensions is a different role from a 60 with one dimension near zero.

## Two kinds of gap

| Hard (scored as real gaps) | Addressable (flagged, not penalized) |
|----------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| Required clearance or license not held | Skill currency: you directed the work but haven't done it hands-on recently |
| Work authorization mismatch | Adjacent experience that transfers with explanation |
| Required degree field entirely absent | Tools you haven't used but whose category you know |
| Regulated domain never touched | Experience you have but didn't put on the CV (fix the CV) |

Addressable gaps get an **"address proactively"** note: a portfolio example, a ready interview answer, or a line naming the adjacent depth. They shouldn't stop you applying. In the author's own search, a decline traced back to exactly this kind of gap: correctly flagged, never addressed. Flagging is half the work; acting on the flag is the other half.

## Scoring discipline

- Score the JD as written, not a charitable reading of it.
- Ambiguous requirement: mark it *partial* and say why.
- Suspected external factors go in the analysis's "External factors" section and never move the score.

## The anchor

Your first scored role becomes the **anchor** (setup step 6): you compare the score with your gut, fix the rubric *definitions* where they disagree, rescore, and lock it in. Every later score is read relative to it, so a 70 means the same thing in month three as in week one.

## Calibration

`/calibrate` compares predictions with outcomes once you have about 15 resolved applications:

| Question | Why |
|----------|-----|
| Do higher score bands convert to screens more often? | Tests whether fit predicts anything for you |
| Do "likely" hypotheses convert more than "coin-flip" ones? | Tests your judgment, not just the math |
| Which sub-scores separate screens from no-screens? | A heavy weight that doesn't separate is a candidate to rethink |
| Screen rate by source | Referrals often beat direct applications regardless of fit |
| Core vs. pivot | Pivots may convert better or worse than their scores suggest |
| Unresolved recruiter-pass fixes at release vs. none | Tests whether the screen check is worth acting on |

It always uses the **frozen** score from the release, not a later revision.

**What the author found:** fit scores were flat against screens above about 40, and source was the stronger signal. The useful change wasn't reweighting the rubric. It was using the score to pick targets and then finding a referral or a warm introduction before applying. Your results may differ, which is the point of measuring.
